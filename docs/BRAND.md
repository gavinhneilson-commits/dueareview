# Due A Review: brand guide

## The idea
**The grade-A stamp.** The "A" in *Due A Review* sits in a tilted orange box, as if it's just been stamped on, like a grade on a school report. It's a nod to the brand's job (handing out grades) and a nice irony: not everything gets an A here.

- **Primary line:** *Honest reviews. No bullshit.* Use it for the tagline, social bios and share images.
- **Hero line:** *If it's brilliant, we'll tell you. If it's shite, we'll tell you that too.* Use it for the homepage and channel trailers.
- **Principle:** *You can't buy a good review. You can only earn one.* Use it for the promise, the footer and press.
- Supporting lines ("Real products. Real testing. Real opinions.", "No hype. No favourites.", "Because your money deserves better.") only appear in the homepage marquee, so the site doesn't drown in slogans.

## Type
- **Archivo**, condensed (width 62–75%) at weight 800–900, uppercase: headlines, section titles, verdicts.
- **Instrument Sans:** body text.
- **JetBrains Mono:** scores, prices, dates, small labels.

All three are open-licence (SIL OFL) and self-hosted, so the site makes no Google Fonts calls (better for privacy and speed).

## Visual language
- Thick ink borders and hard offset shadows (no soft blur, no gradients).
- Score "stamps", not stars. Scores are always shown as `7.4` with the band label.
- Subtle motion only: cards lift, stamps wobble, the marquee drifts. Everything respects "reduce motion".

## Voice
A knowledgeable mate who's actually used the thing. Short sentences. British. Funny when it's earned, never at the expense of the facts. Mild swearing is fine in headlines and verdicts, never in the factual bits. Mock products, not people who bought them.

**Do:** "The '4K' badge is generous. Night footage is mush."
**Don't:** "This is the worst product ever made and anyone who buys it is an idiot."
